More than half of the "conversion problems" people bring me for an audit aren't landing-page problems at all. They're three systemic mistakes that repeat across everyone. "One more block" doesn't fix them — rebuilding the funnel does.

## Mistake 1. A vague hook

Symptom: high traffic, low engagement, lots of bounces. Cause: the headline doesn't name a specific person's pain.

"Boost your sales with AI" is a hook "for everyone" — which means for no one. Who's it addressed to? The owner? A marketer? What "AI" even?

The fix: one segment, one pain, one number. "You're spending 20 hours a week on routine replies — here's how AI takes that off your plate within a month." The primal brain reacts to a concrete "you + 20 hours," not an abstract "boost."

## Mistake 2. An offer "for everyone"

Symptom: leads come in, nobody pays. Cause: the person reached the offer and didn't understand it was about them.

When an offer tries to close a beginner, an expert, and someone "just looking" all at once, it closes none of them. The positioning blurs, and at the moment of decision the brain finds no reason to say "yes."

The fix: an offer for one "ideal" client. Everyone else will come later — through content and touches. Narrowing the offer almost always raises conversion, because relevance goes up.

## Mistake 3. A CTA with no reason

Symptom: people read to the end but don't click. Cause: the button exists, but the reason to click doesn't.

"Submit a request" with no answer to "why right now?" is a request for action into a vacuum. The brain postpones anything with no urgency and no promise of immediate benefit.

The fix: the CTA should promise a benefit and carry a reason: "Book a free funnel review — 30 minutes, 3 spots this week." Value first, then the constraint.

## How to fix it, not patch it

Each of these three mistakes is a symptom of missing a system. Changing a headline is a patch. Rebuilding the funnel around hook → pain → solution → offer → CTA is a system — after which fixes become targeted instead of "start over from scratch."
