Your landing page isn't seen by a person reading it. It's seen by the primal brain — an ancient system that decides in a fraction of a second: "this matters" or "this is noise." If the verdict is "noise," the tab closes before the headline is even read.

We break down how that moment works, and three hooks that make the primal brain stop.

## Why "write a hook" isn't about being clever

Most copy opens with "we're the best company" or a recap of the brand's history. That's a conversation with the new brain — the cortex, responsible for logic. The problem: the cortex only switches on **after** the primal brain has already made its decision. And the primal brain isn't logic — it's threats, novelty, food and reproduction.

A hook is a signal that says "here's something that's personally about you." Not "here's our product," but "here's your problem, and it's solvable."

## Three cognitive traps that do the work for you

**1. The open loop.** The brain holds an unresolved question in focus far more strongly than a resolved one. "Your site brings traffic but doesn't sell. Want to know exactly where 90% of your clients are getting lost?" — that's a loop the reader wants to close.

**2. A concrete number instead of an abstraction.** "We'll boost your conversion" is noise. "+38% leads in 21 days" is tangible. A number is an anchor perception can grab onto.

**3. Visual contrast.** The primal brain doesn't read — it scans. One element on the page needs to visually stand out: color, size, empty space around it. If everything looks the same, nothing does.

## The structure of a 5-second hook

Check your headline against four points:

- **The pain is named** — "you're losing clients at the first touch," not "we offer solutions."
- **Personal address** — "your funnel," not "our expertise."
- **A result is promised** — "here's what to do so this changes."
- **Proof is right there** — a number, a case, a fact. The brain believes what it can verify.
