Put two tiers side by side: "Basic — 5,000 ₽" and "Premium — 5,000 ₽." People will argue over whether they even need the premium one. Now put "Basic — 5,000 ₽," "Premium — 15,000 ₽," and "VIP — 45,000 ₽" next to each other — and premium suddenly looks like a reasonable middle ground, not a splurge. Nothing about the product changed. Only the anchor did.

We break down how a price anchor works, and why an offer without one sells worse even when the product itself is stronger.

## The brain can't judge a price — only compare it

The primal brain has no mechanism for computing a "fair" price in a vacuum. What it does have is a powerful comparison mechanism: expensive or cheap is always expensive or cheap *relative to something*. The first number a buyer sees becomes the reference point for every number after it — that's the anchor.

That's exactly why the expensive tier in a lineup often isn't there to be bought. It's there so the middle tier looks like a smart decision next to it, not a risk.

## Three honest forms of anchoring

**1. Was / now.** "Used to be 25,000 ₽, now 19,900 ₽" — the brain locks onto the size of the gap, not the absolute number. This only works if the "before" price is real, not a crossed-out number invented for the page.

**2. A more expensive alternative.** Not a comparison with a competitor, but with what the person is already doing instead of buying. "Hiring a marketer costs roughly 240,000 ₽/month in salary" next to "subscription — 4,900 ₽/month" is an anchor through opportunity cost, not a feature list.

**3. A three-step ladder.** A low-risk entry point → the core product → a premium tier. Each level becomes the anchor for the one below it. This only works if every level delivers a real, distinct result — not the same value sliced into pieces.

## Where this logic shows up in a real offer

NCAi's "Unboxing" offer is exactly this kind of ladder: a risk-free 190 ₽ assessment → a 19,900 ₽ agency unboxing (the first month's subscription already included) → a 4,900 ₽/mo subscription after that. The first step removes the fear of "what if this doesn't work," the second is the decision point, the third isn't really a decision anymore — it's a continuation of something already working.

The annual subscription at 49,000 ₽ instead of 58,800 ₽ paid monthly follows the same principle: an anchor through comparison, not a generic "discount."

## Where this logic breaks easily

A price anchor collapses the moment even one level feels fake — a "before" price nobody ever actually paid, or a premium tier that obviously exists only for contrast and delivers nothing extra. The brain reads fakeness faster than logic can justify it, and once that happens the whole ladder stops working, not just the one rung.
